#### Insight #1 (Insight1)
*Summary:* Astrocyte-derived fibronectin (FN1) is a proximal mechanistic mediator linking APOE ε4 to blood–brain barrier (BBB) dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease. APOE4 and amyloid-β42/inflammatory signals drive astrocytic FN1 upregulation and amorphous perivascular deposition that accumulates at AQP4-positive endfeet, and FN1 accumulation is sufficient to cause BBB leakage by engaging integrin/FAK signaling to suppress the VEGFA/HB-EGF/IGF-1 trophic axis.

**Question1:** In a study of Alzheimer's disease (AD), APOE ε4/ε4 human astrocytes showed elevated fibronectin (FN1) expression and generated amorphous FN1 deposits, whereas APOE ε3/ε3 astrocytes deposited lower levels of FN1 in fibrillar, thread-like arrays. FN1 strongly colocalized with AQP4 across all postmortem brain samples, confirming enrichment at astrocytic endfeet at the gliovascular interface. Based on these observations, which conclusion is best supported?
A) APOE ε4 carriers have higher perivascular FN1 at AQP4-positive endfeet than APOE ε3 carriers because the ε4 genotype shifts astrocyte FN1 deposition from a fibrillar to an amorphous pattern, which accumulates more readily at the gliovascular interface.
B) APOE ε4 carriers have higher perivascular FN1 at AQP4-positive endfeet because AQP4 expression drives FN1 transcription only in ε4 astrocytes, making the colocalization genotype-specific.
C) APOE ε3 carriers deposit FN1 predominantly at non-endfeet membrane domains, so the AQP4 colocalization seen in ε4 samples reflects a loss of ε3-specific AQP4 distribution rather than ε4-driven FN1 upregulation.
D) APOE ε4 carriers have higher perivascular FN1 because amorphous deposits indicate active proteolytic cleavage of fibrillar FN1, and the cleavage product binds AQP4 more avidly than intact FN1 fibrils.
**Answer1:** A

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** The stem states that ε4/ε4 astrocytes show both higher FN1 expression and a shift to amorphous (rather than fibrillar) deposits, while FN1 colocalizes with AQP4 across all samples. Combining (i) ε4 = higher FN1 expression, (ii) ε4 = amorphous rather than fibrillar morphology, and (iii) colocalization with AQP4 at endfeet, the supported conclusion is that ε4 carriers accumulate more perivascular FN1 at AQP4-positive endfeet because the ε4 genotype shifts astrocytic FN1 to an amorphous pattern that accumulates at the gliovascular interface.
**Why B is wrong:** The reasoning fails: colocalization with AQP4 was observed across all samples (not only ε4), and the data do not support AQP4 driving FN1 transcription in an ε4-specific manner. The conclusion is not licensed.
**Why C is wrong:** The conclusion conflicts with the stated observations: FN1 colocalized with AQP4 across all samples, and lower FN1 in ε3 was paired with fibrillar arrays, not with loss of AQP4 distribution.
**Why D is wrong:** The reasoning invents a proteolytic mechanism not supported by any premise. The stem contrasts amorphous vs. fibrillar morphology but provides no evidence of proteolytic cleavage producing the amorphous form.

**Question2:** In an AD model, astroglial FN1 overexpression in zebrafish was sufficient to cause BBB leakage. In vitro, FN1 acts through integrin/FAK signaling to suppress VEGFA, which reduces endothelial HB-EGF and downstream astrocytic IGF-1. FAK inhibition, integrin β1 blockade with ATN-161, and HB-EGF/IGF-1 supplementation each restored components of this cascade and BBB integrity. A human CSF analysis showed an FN1 × APOE ε4 × disease interaction (F = 14.02, P = 1.81×10⁻⁴). If astrocytic FN1 were experimentally reduced in APOE ε4 carriers with BBB leakage, what outcome does this evidence best predict?
A) BBB integrity would improve, because the zebrafish sufficiency test and the integrin/FAK rescue experiments together indicate that FN1 is a proximal driver whose reduction should restore VEGFA/HB-EGF/IGF-1 signaling and barrier function.
B) BBB integrity would be unchanged, because the CSF interaction analysis only establishes that FN1 levels correlate with disease in ε4 carriers and cannot demonstrate that lowering FN1 is sufficient to restore barrier function.
C) BBB integrity would worsen, because amorphous FN1 deposits are protective and their loss would remove a stabilizing scaffold at AQP4-positive endfeet that compensates for ε4-related trophic deficits.
D) BBB integrity would improve only if FN1 were reduced below ε3 levels, because the ε4 phenotype is defined by amorphous (not fibrillar) FN1 and any residual ε4-pattern FN1 would continue to suppress VEGFA.
**Answer2:** A

**Rubric2:**
**Why correct:** The zebrafish gain-of-function result shows FN1 is sufficient to cause BBB leakage. The integrin/FAK rescue experiments show that interrupting the FN1-initiated cascade restores BBB integrity, implying that lowering FN1 should relieve suppression of VEGFA/HB-EGF/IGF-1. Chaining (i) FN1 overexpression is sufficient for leakage and (ii) blocking the downstream integrin/FAK node rescues barrier function predicts that reducing the proximal node (FN1) in ε4 carriers would likewise restore barrier function. The CSF interaction supports the clinical relevance of FN1 in ε4 AD but is not the sole basis.
**Why B is wrong:** The reasoning conflates an association (CSF interaction) with the experimental evidence: the zebrafish sufficiency and integrin/FAK rescue data directly show that interrupting the FN1-initiated cascade restores BBB integrity, so the counterfactual is answerable in the 'improve' direction.
**Why C is wrong:** The reasoning misreads amorphous FN1 deposits as protective; the stem states they are linked to APOE4-driven BBB dysfunction, and FN1 overexpression causes leakage, contradicting a protective role.
**Why D is wrong:** The conclusion overstates the quantitative threshold: no premise establishes that FN1 must be reduced below ε3 levels. The evidence shows amorphous, ε4-pattern FN1 is the pathogenic form, not that any residual ε4-pattern FN1 necessarily retains full suppressive activity.

#### Insight #2 (Insight2)
*Summary:* In aged (21–22 month) APOE ε3/ε3 versus APOE ε4/ε4 knock-in mice, APOE ε4 drives a robust, genotype-dependent ~99% increase in vascular fibronectin (P = 4.1×10⁻³⁹), coinciding with elevated fibrinogen extravasation (+79.7%, P = 1.7×10⁻³), increased FN1–fibrinogen colocalization, and dextran–Texas Red leakage, collectively indicating APOE4-associated BBB dysfunction independent of overt amyloid pathology.

**Answer1:** B

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** Option B correctly integrates the four stated observations (FN1 increase, fibrinogen extravasation increase, FN1–fibrinogen colocalization, dextran–Texas Red leakage) as orthogonal, independent indicators of vessel-wall compromise in APOE ε4 mice, matching the derivation that the convergent readouts collectively establish APOE4-associated BBB dysfunction.
**Why A is wrong:** Fails because it treats only the fibrinogen readout as sufficient and treats the FN1 change as irrelevant; the derivation explicitly uses FN1 elevation together with fibrinogen extravasation and colocalization, so isolating fibrinogen alone misrepresents the evidence.
**Why C is wrong:** Fails because the data speak to vascular fibronectin/BBB compromise independent of amyloid, not to a reduction of amyloid pathology; the absence of overt AD pathology is used to rule out amyloid as a confounder of the vascular phenotype, not as evidence of amyloid reduction.
**Why D is wrong:** Fails because dextran–Texas Red extravasation is presented as a BBB permeability assay in the derivation; reframing it as a perfusion marker contradicts the stated use of the assay to assess barrier leakage.

**Question2:** Aged APOE ε3/ε3 and APOE ε4/ε4 knock-in mice were compared on four readouts: cortical vascular FN1 (+98.9%, P = 4.1×10⁻³⁹), fibrinogen intensity (+79.7%, P = 1.7×10⁻³), FN1–fibrinogen colocalization, and dextran–Texas Red leakage. If the FN1–fibrinogen colocalization signal were abolished (e.g., by disrupting FN1–fibrinogen binding) while FN1 levels and fibrinogen extravasation remained as observed, which conclusion is best supported by the stated evidence?
A) ApoE genotype would no longer affect BBB function because loss of FN1–fibrinogen overlap would eliminate the only mechanistic link between fibronectin accumulation and fibrinogen extravasation.
B) ApoE genotype would still be associated with elevated vascular FN1 and fibrinogen extravasation because the colocalization readout is an additional orthogonal indicator of barrier compromise rather than the causal bridge between FN1 and fibrinogen in the stated evidence.
C) ApoE genotype would normalize dextran–Texas Red leakage because colocalization of FN1 and fibrinogen is the proximate cause of tracer extravasation in the APOE ε4 vasculature.

**Rubric2:**
**Why correct:** Option B correctly limits the counterfactual to what the stated evidence licenses: the FN1 increase and fibrinogen extravasation are each observed as separate genotype-dependent readouts, and the colocalization is presented as an additional supporting indicator rather than as the established causal bridge that, if removed, would abolish the genotype effect. The evidence does not show that colocalization is the mechanistic link connecting FN1 to barrier leakage.
**Why A is wrong:** Fails because it overreads the colocalization as the single mechanistic link between FN1 and fibrinogen; the derivation treats these as parallel readouts of barrier compromise rather than as a chain in which colocalization is the obligatory step.
**Why C is wrong:** Fails because it elevates colocalization to the proximate cause of dextran leakage; the stated design treats the dextran assay as an independent permeability readout, not as downstream of FN1–fibrinogen binding.
**Why D is wrong:** Fails because the evidence does not license a switch from a fibronectin- to a fibrinogen-driven phenotype on loss of colocalization; both FN1 elevation and fibrinogen extravasation are independently observed in APOE ε4 mice, so neither readout is dependent on the colocalization signal to register a genotype effect.

#### Insight #3 (Insight3)
*Summary:* Reactive astrocytes, particularly the SERPINA3⁺ inflammatory cluster 5, represent a major cellular source of APOE ε4-associated FN1 expression in AD brains. Although astrocytes constitute 22.7% of FN1-expressing cells (less than excitatory neurons at 32.68%), they appear to be the predominant contributors to pathological perivascular FN1 deposition at the gliovascular interface due to their abundance (~40% of brain cells), near-universal vascular contact, and demonstrated APOE ε4-dependent FN1 protein upregulation.

**Question1:** In a single-nucleus RNA-seq analysis of 413,175 cells from 52 individuals, 19,387 cells (4.7%) express FN1. Astrocytes constitute 22.7% of these FN1-expressing cells, whereas excitatory neurons constitute 32.68% and endothelial cells plus pericytes together constitute 8.9%. Astrocytes are ~40% of brain cells and nearly all contact vasculature, whereas endothelial cells and pericytes together are only 2–4% of CNS cells. ROSMAP analysis of 424 DLPFC samples (1.64 million nuclei) links FN1 to astrocyte cluster 5 (SERPINA3⁺, β = 0.0300, FDR P = 1.55×10⁻⁶). Which interpretation is best supported?
A) Excitatory neurons are the predominant source of pathological perivascular FN1 deposition in AD because they account for the largest fraction (32.68%) of FN1-expressing cells in the snRNA-seq data.
B) Astrocytes are likely the predominant contributors to pathological perivascular FN1 deposition in AD because, although they are 22.7% of FN1-expressing cells, their abundance (~40% of brain cells) and near-universal vascular contact indicate a disproportionate role at the gliovascular interface.
C) Endothelial cells and pericytes are the predominant contributors to pathological perivascular FN1 deposition in AD because they jointly constitute only 8.9% of FN1-expressing cells, indicating highly cell-type-specific expression.
D) No cell type can be considered predominant because astrocytes, excitatory neurons, endothelial cells, and pericytes all express FN1, and the fractions are all within the same order of magnitude.
**Answer1:** C

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** The keyed option (C) correctly integrates the stated observations: astrocytes are 22.7% of FN1-expressing cells (lower than excitatory neurons at 32.68%), but their overall abundance (~40% of brain cells), near-universal vascular contact, and strong AD association in cluster 5 (β = 0.0300, FDR P = 1.55×10⁻⁶) together support their predominance at the perivascular ECM interface, not their numerical majority among FN1-expressing cells.
**Why A is wrong:** Right observation (excitatory neurons are the largest fraction of FN1-expressing cells at 32.68%), but wrong conclusion: the question requires integrating cell abundance and perivascular localization, not just the fraction of FN1-expressing cells.
**Why B is wrong:** Right observation (astrocytes are ~40% of brain cells and contact vasculature), but wrong conclusion: a low fraction among FN1-expressing cells does not, on its own, indicate a 'highly cell-type-specific' pattern, and the 8.9% figure does not support endothelial/pericyte predominance.
**Why D is wrong:** Wrong conclusion: the convergence of multiple independent lines (cluster 5 association, perivascular localization, cell abundance, APOE ε4-dependent upregulation) does license a predominant contributor even when no single cell type is a majority of FN1-expressing cells.

**Question2:** Multiplex immunostaining of 1,098,338 cells across genotypes shows that the APOE ε4/ε4 genotype increases FN1 protein levels selectively in astrocytes (165,099 GFAP⁺ astrocytes analyzed). An independent snRNA-seq dataset shows astrocytes display the highest APOE ε4-dependent FN1 upregulation among all cell types. Single-cell transcriptomic studies have reported higher FN1 mRNA in pericytes and endothelial cells than in astrocytes. Given these findings, what is the strongest conclusion about astrocytic FN1 in AD?
A) Astrocytes do not contribute meaningfully to FN1 production in AD because single-cell transcriptomic studies show higher FN1 mRNA in pericytes and endothelial cells than in astrocytes.
B) Astrocytes contribute substantially to APOE ε4-associated pathological FN1 deposition in AD because the APOE ε4/ε4 genotype selectively upregulates astrocytic FN1 protein and astrocytes show the highest APOE ε4-dependent FN1 mRNA upregulation, even though mRNA measurements underestimate astrocytic ECM protein output.
C) Pericytes and endothelial cells are the dominant source of pathological FN1 deposition in AD because their mRNA levels exceed those in astrocytes, and mRNA abundance is the primary determinant of ECM protein levels.
D) The APOE ε4/ε4 genotype increases FN1 protein in all CNS cell types equally because the immunostaining panel included astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, neurons, endothelial cells, and pericytes.
**Answer2:** B

**Rubric2:**
**Why correct:** The keyed option (B) integrates: (1) APOE ε4/ε4 selectively increases astrocytic FN1 protein (immunostaining), (2) astrocytes show the highest APOE ε4-dependent FN1 upregulation (independent snRNA-seq), and (3) mRNA levels and ECM protein deposition can diverge due to post-transcriptional regulation and ECM assembly, which resolves the apparent contradiction with prior single-cell mRNA studies.
**Why A is wrong:** Wrong conclusion: ignores the stated evidence that APOE ε4/ε4 selectively increases astrocytic FN1 protein and that astrocytes show the highest ε4-dependent upregulation, and the explicit caveat that mRNA and ECM protein can diverge.
**Why C is wrong:** Wrong conclusion: assumes mRNA abundance is the primary determinant of ECM protein levels, contradicting the stated caveat that mRNA and deposited ECM protein can diverge substantially.
**Why D is wrong:** Wrong conclusion: the stated finding is that the APOE ε4/ε4 effect is selective for astrocytes, not equal across all cell types.

#### Insight #4 (Insight4)
*Summary:* Inflammation—specifically TNF/NF-κB signaling—is identified by the authors as a major driver of fibronectin (FN1) deposition in reactive astrocytes, with APOE ε4 carriers with AD showing stronger astrocytic NF-κB1 upregulation (P < 0.0001) and NF-κB2 expression strongly predicting astrocytic FN1 levels (β ≈ 0.44, P = 2.4 × 10⁻²¹). Anti-inflammatory interventions rescue amyloid-induced FN1 deposition in vivo.

**Question1:** In a 3D primary human astrocyte hydrogel model, TNF treatment increased expression of reactive markers SERPINA3 and SAA1, activated NF-κB signaling, and upregulated FN1, whereas IL-4 reversed these effects; in postmortem AD cortex, astrocytic NF-κB1 was elevated (P < 0.0001), with a stronger elevation in APOE ε4 carriers, and NF-κB2 expression predicted astrocytic FN1 levels (β ≈ 0.44, P = 2.4 × 10⁻²¹). Given this pattern across the in vitro and snRNA-seq datasets, which conclusion is best supported?
A) TNF activates NF-κB signaling in astrocytes, and the in vivo NF-κB1/NF-κB2–FN1 associations suggest NF-κB signaling contributes to astrocytic FN1 deposition in AD because TNF and IL-4 produced opposing effects on the same FN1-related reactive program in vitro.
B) The stronger NF-κB1 elevation in APOE ε4 carriers proves APOE ε4 is the obligatory upstream cause of FN1 deposition in AD because the carrier effect size exceeded the non-carrier effect size in the snRNA-seq data.
C) IL-4 reduces astrocytic FN1 by directly repressing the FN1 promoter, so the IL-4 effect in vitro is sufficient to conclude that anti-inflammatory interventions will lower FN1 in APOE ε4 AD brains because the in vitro system used the same APOE genotype.
D) The NF-κB2–FN1 association (β ≈ 0.44) is stronger evidence for causation than the NF-κB1 APOE ε4 interaction because a smaller P-value necessarily indicates a more upstream position in the signaling cascade.
**Answer1:** A

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** Option A combines two premises: (1) TNF induces NF-κB signaling and FN1 in vitro while IL-4 opposes this, and (2) NF-κB1/NF-κB2 expression in vivo correlates with astrocytic FN1 in AD. The opposing cytokine effects in the same in vitro reactive program show the FN1 response is inflammatory-regulated, and the in vivo associations are consistent with that mechanism — together supporting inflammation/NF-κB as a contributor to astrocytic FN1 deposition.
**Why B is wrong:** Fails on both parts: 'obligatory upstream cause' overstates the evidence (the snRNA-seq data show association, not necessity), and the reasoning assumes a larger carrier effect size proves causation rather than just stronger association.
**Why C is wrong:** Fails on reasoning: the in vitro system does not use the same APOE genotype as the AD carriers, and IL-4 reducing FN1 in vitro does not establish direct promoter repression or clinical efficacy in APOE ε4 AD brains.
**Why D is wrong:** Fails on reasoning: a smaller P-value does not indicate upstream cascade position — it reflects effect size, variance, and sample size. The 'stronger evidence for causation' framing also misreads a statistical association as causal proof.

**Question2:** In a zebrafish model, TNF injection induced fibronectin deposition around astroglia concomitant with NF-κB:GFP activation, and treatment with camptothecin or Torin2 reversed amyloid-induced fibronectin deposition and astrocytic hypertrophy at the gliovascular interface. If a researcher were to repeat the zebrafish experiment but co-inject TNF with a NF-κB inhibitor, which outcome would be best supported by the reported findings?
A) NF-κB inhibition would abolish TNF-induced fibronectin deposition because TNF-induced FN1 deposition in the zebrafish model was observed alongside NF-κB activation, consistent with NF-κB being sufficient for the fibronectin response.
B) NF-κB inhibition would have no effect on TNF-induced fibronectin because TNF can signal through NF-κB-independent pathways, so blocking NF-κB would leave FN1 unchanged regardless of the co-activation observed.
C) NF-κB inhibition would increase fibronectin deposition because the inhibitor would disrupt a negative feedback loop, so removing NF-κB signaling would amplify TNF-driven FN1 beyond what TNF alone produces.
D) NF-κB inhibition would prove that amyloid-induced fibronectin deposition is obligatory because camptothecin and Torin2 already rescued that phenotype, and any NF-κB effect must be the only route in the zebrafish model.
**Answer2:** A

**Rubric2:**
**Why correct:** The stem states that TNF injection induced fibronectin deposition 'concomitant with NF-κB:GFP activation,' establishing that NF-κB activation co-occurs with the FN1 response. This co-occurrence plus the in vitro evidence that TNF activates NF-κB signaling while inducing FN1 supports NF-κB as sufficient for the fibronectin response. Blocking NF-κB would therefore be expected to abolish the TNF-induced deposition under this sufficiency framing.
**Why B is wrong:** Fails on reasoning: the stated co-activation specifically links NF-κB to the FN1 response in this model; claiming NF-κB-independent signaling would dominate ignores the premise that NF-κB activation accompanies the FN1 induction.
**Why C is wrong:** Fails on conclusion and reasoning: nothing in the stem supports a negative-feedback model; increased deposition upon NF-κB inhibition contradicts the co-activation observation.
**Why D is wrong:** Fails on both parts: camptothecin/Torin2 act on microglial polarization and are not specific NF-κB inhibitors, so the rescue does not establish NF-κB as the obligatory route, and the claim of obligatory status overstates sufficiency as necessity.

#### Insight #5 (Insight5)

#### Insight #6 (Insight6)
*Summary:* Fibronectin suppresses astrocytic VEGFA ligand expression through integrin-mediated focal adhesion kinase (FAK) activity, with VEGFA reduced by 12.3% in AD brains (P = 3.7×10⁻¹⁶), by 19.4% in APOE ε4/ε4 astrocytes (P = 3.6×10⁻¹⁰), and by 25.7% upon FN1 coating (P = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰); pharmacological blockade of FAK (PF-573228) or β-integrin (ATN-161, +20.0%, P = 1.0×10⁻⁴) restores VEGFA, establishing FN1–integrin–FAK signaling as the upstream suppressor of the VEGF/HB-EGF/IGF-1 axis at the gliovascular interface.

**Question1:** In a zebrafish model, single-cell transcriptomics of 32,294 cells across 38 clusters comparing wild-type and fn1b knockout under Aβ42 conditions found that astrocytes in the knockout upregulated vegfa, with KEGG enrichment of VEGF (P = 3.8×10⁻², OR = 2.87), insulin (P = 4.3×10⁻³, OR = 35.78), and MAPK (P = 4.0×10⁻⁵, OR = 3.41) pathways. In human samples, VEGFA protein was 12.3% lower in AD versus control postmortem brains (P = 3.7×10⁻¹⁶) and 19.4% lower in APOE ε4/ε4 versus isogenic ε3/ε3 iPSC-derived astrocytes (P = 3.6×10⁻¹⁰). Culturing ε3/ε3 astrocytes on fibronectin-coated plates reduced VEGFA by 25.7% (P = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰). Which comparison best isolates fibronectin-integrin signaling as an upstream suppressor of astrocytic VEGFA?
A) The −25.7% fibronectin-coating effect in ε3/ε3 astrocytes versus the −19.4% APOE ε4/ε4 baseline effect, because fibronectin coating is a defined intervention that phenocopies the genetic effect without introducing a new pathway.
B) The KEGG ORs of 2.87 (VEGF), 35.78 (insulin), and 3.41 (MAPK), because the largest OR identifies the most strongly enriched pathway in fn1b knockout astrocytes.
C) The −12.3% AD-brain effect versus the −19.4% ε4/ε4 effect, because both are reductions in VEGFA protein and therefore the in vivo human phenotype is identical in magnitude to the cell-model phenotype.
D) The fn1b knockout itself versus Aβ42 treatment, because any gene upregulated in fn1b⁻/⁻ astrocytes must be directly suppressed by fibronectin-integrin signaling under physiological conditions.
**Answer1:** A

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** Option A correctly identifies the −25.7% fibronectin-coating effect as a defined intervention that converts ε3/ε3 astrocytes toward the ε4/ε4-like VEGFA reduction, showing that extracellular fibronectin is sufficient to drive the suppression. This isolates the FN1-integrin-FAK axis as upstream of VEGFA without confounding variables. The reasoning chains two premises: (1) the ε3/ε3 baseline is a defined cellular state, and (2) adding fibronectin coating moves VEGFA downward by a fraction comparable to the ε4/ε4 genotype, meaning fibronectin signaling alone is sufficient to mimic the genetic APOE ε4 effect.
**Why B is wrong:** The conclusion is wrong. The magnitude of a KEGG OR reflects enrichment of differentially expressed genes within a pathway, not which pathway is 'most strongly' regulated by fibronectin-integrin signaling upstream of VEGFA. Comparing ORs across different pathways does not identify a causal suppressor; it identifies the most transcriptionally altered pathway. The reasoning fails by conflating pathway enrichment size with upstream causal position.
**Why C is wrong:** The conclusion that the in vivo and cell-model phenotypes are 'identical in magnitude' is wrong. The two effects have different baselines (postmortem brain tissue vs. iPSC-derived astrocytes from different genotypes) and different denominators, so the −12.3% and −19.4% cannot be directly compared or equated. The reasoning fails by combining percentages from different reference groups as if they shared a baseline.
