#### Insight #1 (Insight1)
*Summary:* In aged female and male mice, senescence and DAM gene expression was more pronounced in fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum white matter than in hippocampal gray matter. Age-dependent increases in these transcriptional signatures were observed more consistently in white matter than in the hippocampus or cerebellum, emphasizing white matter susceptibility to late-life senescence and DAM changes.

**Question1:** In aged female and male mice, RT-PCR measurements of p16ink4a, Cdkn2a, Lgals3, Cdkn1a/p21, and Ccl2 in microdissected fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum white matter were significantly higher than in hippocampal gray matter. Which conclusion is best supported by these cross-regional measurements?
A) Aged white matter shows more pronounced senescence and pro-inflammatory gene expression than aged hippocampal gray matter, because the same set of transcripts was significantly elevated in fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum relative to hippocampus within the same aged animals.
B) Aged hippocampal gray matter is transcriptionally more inflamed than white matter, because the measurements only sampled aged mice and the white-matter elevations could reflect baseline regional differences.
C) These genes are specific markers of white-matter aging and not expressed in gray matter, because every measured transcript was significantly higher in fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum than in hippocampus.
D) The white-matter elevations prove that microglia in fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum are the unique cellular source of the measured transcripts, because these genes are known DAM and senescence markers.
**Answer1:** A

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** The cross-regional comparison within the same aged animals shows p16ink4a, Cdkn2a, Lgals3, Cdkn1a/p21, and Ccl2 were each significantly higher in fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum than in hippocampus. The conclusion is that aged white matter exhibits more pronounced senescence and pro-inflammatory transcriptional signatures than aged hippocampal gray matter, and the reasoning correctly ties this to the within-animal, across-region direction of the differences.
**Why B is wrong:** Wrong conclusion (inverts direction). The reasoning attempts to dismiss the white-matter elevation by appealing to a missing young control, but the stated evidence already establishes that these transcripts were significantly higher in white matter than hippocampus within the aged cohort.
**Why C is wrong:** Wrong conclusion (overclaims exclusivity). A higher expression in white matter does not mean the genes are absent in gray matter; the data only show relative elevation, and the genes were detected (measured) in hippocampal tissue as part of the comparison.
**Why D is wrong:** Wrong conclusion (overclaims cellular attribution). The measurements are bulk tissue RT-PCR from microdissected regions, not isolated microglia, so they cannot localize the transcripts to microglia, let alone uniquely to white-matter microglia.

**Question2:** Relative to young, sex-matched mice, p16ink4a, Cdkn2a, Csf1r, Gpr34, and Trem2 increased in both gray and white matter microdissections of old male and female mice, while Apoe, Bcl2a1a, Cd11b, Klk8, Lgals3, Cdkn1a/p21, and Tmem173 increased in old female and male fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum, and Ccl2, Ccl5, Il1b, and Tyrobp increased in old male fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum. A regional heat map showed age-dependent increases more consistently in old fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum than in hippocampus or cerebellum. Given this pattern, what does the evidence most directly license?
A) White matter is selectively vulnerable to late-life senescence and DAM transcriptional changes, because the white-matter regions showed more age-related transcripts and a broader age-induced gene set than hippocampus or cerebellum.
B) Hippocampus and cerebellum are protected from aging-related transcriptional changes, because fewer transcripts increased with age in those regions than in fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum.
C) Male mice age differently from female mice in every white-matter region, because Ccl2, Ccl5, Il1b, and Tyrobp increased only in old male fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum.
D) The brain shows a uniform aging transcriptional signature across all regions, because some genes (p16ink4a, Cdkn2a, Csf1r, Gpr34, Trem2) increased in both gray and white matter.
**Answer2:** A

**Rubric2:**
**Why correct:** Two premises must be chained: (1) more age-dependent transcripts increased in fimbria-fornix and corpus callosum than in hippocampus or cerebellum, and (2) a broader age-induced gene set appeared in white matter. Together these support the conclusion that white matter is selectively susceptible to late-life senescence/DAM transcriptional changes, without overclaiming a proven mechanistic gate.
**Why B is wrong:** Wrong conclusion. 'Fewer transcripts increased' is consistent with a smaller or different age response, not with full protection; the evidence does not establish that hippocampus/cerebellum are immune to aging changes.
**Why C is wrong:** Wrong conclusion (overclaims). Four transcripts increasing only in males shows sex-specific additions in male white matter, not that every white-matter region differs between sexes.
**Why D is wrong:** Wrong conclusion. A shared subset of genes rising in both gray and white matter does not imply uniformity, since an additional, broader set rose in white matter only — the opposite of a uniform signature.

**Why correct:** Option C correctly states both observations given in the stem: (1) the cross-regional comparison identified Fth1, Ctss, Apoe (established senescence/DAM) plus Ccno and Sesn3 (candidate/emergent) as enriched in fimbria IBA1+ versus hippocampal IBA1+ cells, and (2) within the fimbria Apoe was the most abundant transcript distinguishing IBA1+GAL3+ from IBA1+GAL3− microglia. Apoe abundance in the GAL3+ comparison does not prove GAL3+ cells are the source of the fimbria-vs-hippocampus enrichment, and the authors explicitly note DSP limitations for phenotyping heterogeneous populations.
