#### Insight #1 (Insight1)
*Summary:* Astrocyte-derived fibronectin (FN1) is a proximal mechanistic mediator linking APOE ε4 to blood–brain barrier (BBB) dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease. APOE4 and amyloid-β42/inflammatory signals drive astrocytic FN1 upregulation and amorphous perivascular deposition that accumulates at AQP4-positive endfeet, and FN1 accumulation is sufficient to cause BBB leakage by engaging integrin/FAK signaling to suppress the VEGFA/HB-EGF/IGF-1 trophic axis.

**Question1:** In postmortem brain tissue, APOE ε4/ε4 astrocytes showed higher FN1 expression and produced amorphous perivascular deposits, whereas APOE ε3/ε3 astrocytes produced lower FN1 levels organized in fibrillar, thread-like arrays. FN1 strongly colocalized with AQP4 at astrocytic endfeet. Based on these observations, which interpretation is most consistent with the stated pattern?
A) APOE ε4 status is associated with both increased astrocytic FN1 and a shift toward amorphous perivascular deposits at AQP4-positive endfeet, because the same ε4/ε4 cells that show elevated FN1 also display the amorphous organization at the gliovascular interface.
B) The amorphous deposits in ε4/ε4 brains are produced primarily by vascular endothelial cells, because perivascular accumulation at the BBB implies a vascular origin.
C) APOE ε3/ε3 astrocytes lack meaningful fibronectin at the gliovascular interface, because their deposits appear fibrillar and thread-like rather than amorphous.
D) FN1/AQP4 colocalization shows that AQP4 is the upstream driver of fibronectin deposition, because colocalization at endfeet implies that AQP4 induces FN1 accumulation.
**Answer1:** A

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** The keyed option correctly integrates two paired observations: (i) ε4/ε4 astrocytes show both elevated FN1 levels and an amorphous (rather than fibrillar) deposit organization, and (ii) FN1 colocalizes with AQP4 at endfeet, placing the deposits at the gliovascular interface. The conclusion (ε4 status is associated with both quantitative and qualitative changes in perivascular FN1 at AQP4-positive endfeet) and the reasoning (same cells, same location) are both supported by the stated findings.
**Why B is wrong:** Fails on conclusion. The stated evidence explicitly attributes FN1 deposition to astrocytes ('astrocytes drive APOE ε4-dependent FN1 deposition'), not endothelial cells. The premise that perivascular location implies vascular origin is an unsupported inference.
**Why C is wrong:** Fails on conclusion. The ε3/ε3 astrocytes are described as depositing fibronectin at 'lower levels' in fibrillar arrays — not as lacking fibronectin at the interface. The reasoning (qualitative morphology implies absence) overstates the stated quantitative difference.
**Why D is wrong:** Fails on reasoning and conclusion direction. Colocalization is consistent spatial overlap, not causation. The stated evidence places FN1 downstream of APOE4/amyloid-β/inflammatory signals, not downstream of AQP4, and there is no stated manipulation showing AQP4 drives FN1.

**Question2:** In vivo, astroglial overexpression of FN1 in zebrafish was sufficient to cause BBB leakage. In cell and vascular models, FN1 engaged integrin/FAK signaling and suppressed VEGFA, which reduced endothelial HB-EGF and downstream astrocytic IGF-1. FAK inhibition, integrin β1 blockade (ATN-161), and HB-EGF or IGF-1 supplementation each restored components of BBB integrity. If FN1's effect on the VEGFA → HB-EGF → IGF-1 axis were absent, which conclusion would the stated evidence still license?
A) FN1 would no longer be sufficient to cause BBB leakage, because the in vivo zebrafish leakiness requires suppression of the trophic cascade as the obligate effector.
B) FN1 overexpression would still be sufficient to cause BBB leakage in zebrafish, because the in vivo sufficiency result is independent of any particular downstream pathway once FN1 is elevated above a threshold.
C) FN1 would still be sufficient to cause BBB leakage in zebrafish, because the in vivo result directly links FN1 elevation to leakiness without identifying a specific required intermediate, and the rescue experiments were performed in vitro and in vivo only for the integrin/FAK and growth-factor arms.
D) FN1 would no longer drive BBB leakage, because the integrin/FAK signaling step is required to translate FN1 accumulation into any vascular phenotype.
**Answer2:** C

**Rubric2:**
**Why correct:** The in vivo zebrafish experiment showed that FN1 overexpression is sufficient for BBB leakage on its own; the rescue experiments (FAK inhibition, integrin β1 blockade, HB-EGF/IGF-1 supplementation) demonstrated that each of these components can restore integrity, which shows the integrin/FAK → VEGFA → HB-EGF → IGF-1 axis is involved — but the in vivo sufficiency of FN1 was demonstrated independently of any single required intermediate. Removing the trophic cascade does not negate the stated sufficiency result, since the leakiness phenotype was observed upon FN1 elevation alone. The conclusion (FN1 remains sufficient) and the reasoning (the in vivo result does not single out one obligate intermediate, and rescues were partial within specific arms) are both consistent with the stated evidence.
**Why A is wrong:** Fails on conclusion. The stated zebrafish experiment already shows FN1 sufficiency for leakage without isolating a single obligate effector; removing the trophic cascade is not shown to abolish that sufficiency. The reasoning also conflates 'involvement' with 'obligation'.
**Why B is wrong:** Right conclusion (FN1 still sufficient) but wrong reasoning. The premise that 'sufficiency is independent of downstream pathway once FN1 is elevated above a threshold' is not stated; the evidence licenses sufficiency given the manipulation performed, not a general threshold claim about any downstream perturbation.
**Why D is wrong:** Fails on conclusion. FAK inhibition and integrin β1 blockade each restored components of BBB integrity, showing involvement — not that integrin/FAK is the only route. The reasoning overstates necessity from rescue data.

#### Insight #2 (Insight2)
*Summary:* In aged (21–22 month) APOE ε3/ε3 versus APOE ε4/ε4 knock-in mice, APOE ε4 drives a robust, genotype-dependent ~99% increase in vascular fibronectin (P = 4.1×10⁻³⁹), coinciding with elevated fibrinogen extravasation (+79.7%, P = 1.7×10⁻³), increased FN1–fibrinogen colocalization, and dextran–Texas Red leakage, collectively indicating APOE4-associated BBB dysfunction independent of overt amyloid pathology.

**Question1:** In aged (21–22-month-old) APOE ε3 and APOE ε4 knock-in mice, cortical vessels were immunolabeled for fibronectin (FN1) and fibrinogen, and leakage was tested with dextran–Texas Red. Compared with APOE ε3, APOE ε4 vessels showed: FN1 intensity higher by +98.9% (95% CI 84.1–113.7%; P = 4.1×10⁻³⁹); fibrinogen intensity elevated by +79.7% (P = 1.7×10⁻³); significantly increased FN1–fibrinogen colocalization; and increased dextran–Texas Red extravasation. Which conclusion is best supported by this pattern of orthogonal readouts?
A) The data show that APOE ε4 produces a leaky BBB, because the +98.9% FN1 difference alone establishes that BBB disruption is more severe in ε4 than ε3 carriers.
B) The data show that APOE4 is associated with fibronectin-mediated BBB dysfunction in aged mice, because three independent readouts — elevated vessel FN1, elevated fibrinogen extravasation, and increased dextran–Texas Red leakage — all shift in the same direction in ε4 animals.
C) The data show that APOE ε4 causes amyloid-independent neurodegeneration, because the genotype effect on FN1 is large enough to imply downstream neuronal loss in the absence of plaques.
D) The data show that fibronectin is unnecessary for BBB breakdown in ε4 mice, because fibrinogen extravasation increased by +79.7% while FN1 was unchanged between genotypes.
**Answer1:** B

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** Option B correctly concludes that APOE4 is associated with fibronectin-mediated BBB dysfunction in aged mice. The reasoning chains three independent observations — elevated vessel FN1 (+98.9%), elevated fibrinogen extravasation (+79.7%), and increased dextran–Texas Red leakage — all shifting in the same direction in ε4 animals. This convergence of orthogonal readouts supports a BBB-dysfunction interpretation, consistent with the authors' framing that fibronectin accumulation correlates with BBB compromise.
**Why A is wrong:** Fails on reasoning: the +98.9% FN1 elevation is one readout of fibronectin deposition, not a direct measure of BBB leakage. Concluding 'leaky BBB' from FN1 alone over-interprets a single readout; the leakage claim requires the dextran and fibrinogen readouts.
**Why C is wrong:** Fails on conclusion: no neuronal or amyloid-pathology endpoints were measured in the stated premises, so concluding 'amyloid-independent neurodegeneration' introduces evidence not in the stem.
**Why D is wrong:** Fails on both premise and reasoning: the stem explicitly states FN1 is markedly higher in ε4 (+98.9%), not unchanged. The option fabricates a 'FN1 unchanged' claim and then misuses a sufficiency-style logic to deny fibronectin's role.

**Question2:** Aged (21–22-month) APOE ε3 and APOE ε4 knock-in mice were compared at the animal level (GEEs). APOE ε4 vs ε3 showed: FN1 +98.9% (P = 4.1×10⁻³⁹), fibrinogen intensity +79.7% (P = 1.7×10⁻³), and significantly increased FN1–fibrinogen colocalization. If a researcher argued that 'the +79.7% fibrinogen increase proves that BBB breakdown is the primary cause of the +98.9% FN1 elevation,' what is the strongest counter to this claim from the stated evidence?
A) The argument is not supported, because fibrinogen extravasation is a consequence of BBB leakiness rather than a demonstrated cause of FN1 elevation, and the stated evidence shows correlation (colocalization) rather than a causal direction between fibrinogen and FN1.
B) The argument is supported, because the fibrinogen increase is much larger than the FN1 increase when expressed as a ratio of the ε3 baseline, which establishes fibrinogen as the upstream driver.
C) The argument is not supported, because the two percentages were measured against different reference groups (vessels vs. mice) and therefore cannot be compared or combined into a single causal statement.
D) The argument is supported, because the FN1–fibrinogen colocalization test formally proves that fibrinogen deposition drives fibronectin accumulation in ε4 vessels.
**Answer2:** A

**Rubric2:**
**Why correct:** Option A correctly counters the claim. The reasoning is: fibrinogen extravasation is interpreted in the derivation as an indicator of BBB dysfunction (i.e., a downstream consequence of leakiness), and the colocalization analysis establishes spatial overlap, not causal direction. Therefore the evidence licenses an association between FN1, fibrinogen, and BBB compromise, not a causal arrow from fibrinogen → FN1. The stated evidence does not include a manipulation that places fibrinogen upstream of FN1.
**Why B is wrong:** Fails on reasoning: the +79.7% and +98.9% figures share a common reference (APOE ε3), but a larger percent change in fibrinogen does not establish causal primacy. Larger effect size is not evidence of upstream position; P-values/effect sizes reflect variance and n, not pathway order.
**Why C is wrong:** Fails on premise: both percentages are measured against the APOE ε3 reference within the same cortical-vessel imaging paradigm, so the 'different reference groups' objection misreads the design. The correct objection is the causal-direction one in A, not a baseline-mismatch one.
**Why D is wrong:** Fails on conclusion and reasoning: colocalization is a measure of spatial overlap, not a causal test. It does not establish that fibrinogen 'drives' fibronectin accumulation.

#### Insight #3 (Insight3)
*Summary:* Reactive astrocytes, particularly the SERPINA3⁺ inflammatory cluster 5, represent a major cellular source of APOE ε4-associated FN1 expression in AD brains. Although astrocytes constitute 22.7% of FN1-expressing cells (less than excitatory neurons at 32.68%), they appear to be the predominant contributors to pathological perivascular FN1 deposition at the gliovascular interface due to their abundance (~40% of brain cells), near-universal vascular contact, and demonstrated APOE ε4-dependent FN1 protein upregulation.

**Question1:** In an snRNA-seq dataset of 413,175 cells from 52 individuals, astrocytes constitute 22.7% of 19,387 FN1-expressing cells, while endothelial cells and pericytes together constitute 8.9%. Astrocytes comprise ~40% of brain cells and nearly all contact vasculature; endothelial cells and pericytes together account for 2–4% of CNS cells. Given these cell-type fractions and perivascular abundance, which conclusion is best supported?
A) Excitatory neurons are the predominant source of FN1 at the gliovascular interface because they represent the largest fraction of FN1-expressing cells (32.68%), and cell-type proportion alone determines ECM contribution. because larger cell counts among FN1⁺ cells imply more total transcript per cell type, which scales directly with deposited protein.
B) Astrocytes are likely the predominant contributors to pathological perivascular FN1 deposition despite not having the highest fraction of FN1-expressing cells, because their high overall abundance (~40%) and near-universal vascular contact make them the most numerous cells positioned to deposit ECM at the gliovascular interface. because perivascular abundance is a critical determinant of who deposits matrix at that niche.
C) Astrocytes are unlikely to contribute meaningfully to pathological FN1 deposition because they make up only 22.7% of FN1-expressing cells, and any non-astrocyte source (e.g., pericytes) with even modest per-cell expression must dominate. because the fraction of FN1⁺ cells directly reflects per-cell production rates across types.
D) Endothelial cells and pericytes are the predominant FN1 contributors because they collectively account for 8.9% of FN1-expressing cells versus astrocytes' 22.7%, and cells with lower expressing fractions cannot dominate ECM deposition. because a lower expressing fraction rules out a meaningful contribution to protein deposition.
**Answer1:** B

**Rubric1:**
**Why correct:** The stem states astrocytes are 22.7% of FN1-expressing cells (less than excitatory neurons at ~32.68%) but constitute ~40% of brain cells and nearly all contact vasculature. The conclusion that astrocytes are likely predominant contributors to perivascular FN1 deposition follows from combining (i) their high overall abundance with (ii) near-universal vascular contact — two premises that jointly position them as the most numerous perivascular FN1 source, even though their share of FN1⁺ cells is not the highest. Option B correctly states both the conclusion and the reasoning.
**Why A is wrong:** Wrong conclusion and wrong reasoning: claims excitatory neurons dominate deposition based on highest FN1⁺ fraction, but ignores their perivascular scarcity and that mRNA-expressing fraction does not equal ECM deposition contribution. The reasoning equates cell count among FN1⁺ cells with per-cell production and ignores anatomical localization.
**Why C is wrong:** Wrong conclusion and wrong reasoning: 22.7% is not 'only' a small fraction — it is the second-largest FN1⁺ fraction, and the reasoning misreads expressing fraction as per-cell production rate, ignoring that astrocytes are ~40% of brain cells and contact vasculature.
**Why D is wrong:** Wrong conclusion and wrong reasoning: inverts the logic by asserting a lower expressing fraction rules out contribution, and ignores that endothelial cells/pericytes together are only 2–4% of CNS cells, so even a higher expressing fraction per cell may not match astrocyte numbers at the gliovascular interface.

In an independent snRNA-seq dataset, astrocytes show the highest APOE ε4-dependent FN1 upregulation. snRNA-seq also shows astrocyte cluster 5 (SERPINA3⁺, inflammatory) is most strongly associated with FN1 in AD (β = 0.0300, FDR P = 1.55×10⁻⁶). B) The data cannot predict the level of astrocytic FN1 without the SERPINA3⁺ subset, because the only stated ε4-associated upregulation evidence localizes to cluster 5, and no measurement of non-cluster-5 astrocytes under ε4/ε4 is provided. because the evidence is subset-specific and no broader upregulation is demonstrated.

No data are provided on FN1 levels in non-cluster-5 astrocytes under ε4/ε4, so removing that subset eliminates the very cell population in which the ε4-associated signal was observed. The counterfactual therefore cannot be answered from the stated evidence — a legitimate response is to say so. Option B correctly identifies this evidentiary gap.
**Why A is wrong:** Wrong conclusion: asserts FN1 would remain elevated, but the evidence localizes the ε4-associated upregulation to reactive/SERPINA3⁺ astrocytes and does not demonstrate upregulation in non-reactive astrocytes under ε4/ε4. The reasoning over-generalizes from 'astrocytes' to 'all astrocytes.'
**Why C is wrong:** Wrong conclusion and wrong reasoning: overstates the evidence by claiming cluster 5 is the sole FN1 source; the stem states FN1 is produced by multiple cell types (astrocytes, endothelial cells, pericytes, excitatory neurons).
